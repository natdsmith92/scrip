Important — About Screen Color Accuracy

        
Please read before making your selections

      

    


    

      
Please note: Fabric colors viewed on a screen are always approximate. The shades displayed here are digital representations and will look different from the actual woven textile you hold in your hands. Use this poll to indicate your relative preference between the options shown — final color approval should always be confirmed against a physical fabric swatch under controlled lighting conditions.

    


    

      

        

        
Display Technology Varies

        
An iPhone's OLED screen renders deeper blacks and more saturated colors than a typical laptop LCD. OLED, IPS, TN, and AMOLED displays all have different color ranges, contrast levels, and white-point settings. Two people viewing this exact same page on different devices will see noticeably different shades.

      

      

        

        
Brightness & Ambient Lighting

        
Your screen brightness setting and the light in your room both change how you perceive color. Warm overhead lighting adds a yellow tint, while cool fluorescent lights shift colors blue. For the best comparison, view this page in a neutrally-lit room at about 50–70% screen brightness, and avoid direct sunlight on your screen.

      

      

        

        
Fabric Is Three-Dimensional

        
Woven kinesiology tape has real, physical texture — tiny thread ridges and crossings that catch light differently at every angle. A flat image on a screen collapses all of that into a single plane. When you hold the actual fabric, it will appear lighter where light hits the surface and deeper in the shadows between threads, creating a richness no screen can fully show.

      

      

        

        
Pantone Colors & Production Dyes

        
Production fabrics are dyed to specific Pantone color references under standardized D65 lighting. Your screen uses RGB light (red, green, blue mixed together), while physical fabric dyes work by absorbing light — these are fundamentally different color systems. A Pantone-matched fabric swatch viewed in a professional light booth is the only reliable way to approve the final production color.

      

    


    

      💡
      
Best Practice: If possible, request a physical strike-off or drawdown sample from the mill before giving final approval. Compare the swatches under both natural daylight (D65) and retail store lighting (TL84 / F11) to make sure the color looks right in the environments where the product will actually be sold and used.
